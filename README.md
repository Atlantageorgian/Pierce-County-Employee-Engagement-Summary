# Pierce-County-Employee-Engagement-Summary
## Overview
This dashboard summarizes results from the Pierce County Employee Engagement Survey, based on approximately 14,000 employee responses across departments and roles. The purpose of the analysis is to understand current engagement levels, identify key strengths and gaps, and support leadership with clear, data-driven insights to improve employee experience and performance.

## Tools Used and Analysis Process
The analysis was carried out using Power BI, applying the following tools and techniques in line with project requirements and best practices.
- Power Query was used to clean and transform the dataset. This included removing duplicate records, handling missing values, standardizing text fields such as department names, roles, and survey responses, and creating additional columns needed for analysis.
- Data Cleaning focused on fixing inconsistent entries, grouping similar response values, and replacing null or unclear data with meaningful categories such as “Unknown” where appropriate. This ensured consistency and accuracy across the dataset.
- SQL was used to support data validation and aggregation tasks, such as checking response counts, validating department-level totals, and confirming calculated metrics before visualization.
- Data Modeling was implemented using a star schema, with a central fact table containing survey responses and supporting dimension tables for Date, Department, Role, and Survey Questions. This structure improved performance and ensured reliable analysis.
- DAX measures were created to calculate key metrics including the satisfaction index, average engagement scores, agreement and disagreement percentages, and comparisons across departments and roles.
- Data Visualization techniques were applied to clearly present trends, comparisons, and distributions using KPIs, bar charts, and comparison visuals.
This structured approach ensured the data was reliable, consistent, and suitable for executive-level reporting and decision-making.


## Key Findings and Visual Explanations
### Department-Level Engagement Analysis
Engagement levels vary across departments. Family Justice and Emergency Management show relatively stronger engagement scores, while Human Resources and Communications-related departments report lower averages. These differences suggest that employee experience is not uniform and that some departments may benefit from targeted engagement efforts.

### Survey Question Insights
Survey’s responses show that employees generally have a clear understanding of their roles and responsibilities. However, lower agreement levels are seen in areas related to recognition, accountability, and leadership support. These results point to opportunities for improvement at the organizational and management level rather than individual performance issues.

### Role-Based Engagement Comparison
When engagement is viewed by role, a clear pattern emerges. Directors and Managers report higher engagement scores compared to Supervisors and Staff. This suggests a gap between leadership perception and frontline employee experience, highlighting the need for stronger communication, feedback, and support across all levels.

### Staff vs Leadership Engagement
A direct comparison shows that leadership engagement (3.2) is slightly higher than staff engagement (3.0). While the difference is not large, it reinforces the need to ensure leadership decisions and strategies reflect the day-to-day experiences of staff.

## Recommendations
To improve engagement across the organization, the following actions are recommended:
-	Introducing clearer and more consistent employee recognition practices
-	Strengthen feedback and accountability at the management level
-	Apply targeted engagement strategies for lower-performing departments
-	Track staff engagement regularly to measure improvement over time
## Conclusion
This dashboard provides a clear view of employee engagement across Pierce County. While overall engagement is moderate, the findings show clear opportunities to improve recognition, leadership alignment, and staff experience. Addressing these areas can support better employee satisfaction, stronger performance, and long-term organizational success.
